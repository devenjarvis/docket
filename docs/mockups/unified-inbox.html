C · How it sorts A few rules you can read, not a score you have to trust. Every row says which one put it there.

Finish what’s nearly done. Then unblock people. Then read.

A pull request of yours that’s green but not merged, or a review you’ve answered but not posted, takes a minute to finish, and someone waits until you do. Those come first. Then the reviews only you can unblock. Then the reading nobody is waiting for.

The rules, in order

  1. Group by how long it takes. Quick: a minute or two, and something merges or someone stops waiting. Merge, check a fix the agent held, re-review what an author changed for you, reply, nudge. Needs a closer look: a review with questions, or a conflict on yours. Skim and approve: nothing to decide.
  2. Then who’s waiting. First what merges when you act (it’s yours and ready, or your answers are in). Then a review by someone who’s reviewing one of yours. Then anyone who has waited more than a day.
  3. Then the shortest, by the agent’s estimate of your time.
  4. Then the oldest request.

What moves a row

  1. Up to Quick: an author pushes and your answers still apply (you only look at what changed); someone approves yours and the checks pass; nobody has touched a review request on yours for two days (ping them, or ask someone else).
  2. Over to Waiting on others: your agent is reading or fixing it; checks are running; a reviewer hasn’t answered yet; you snoozed it.
  3. Not while you’re looking. New rows are marked, and the order settles when you come back to the list.

Left out on purpose: who the author is, labels like “urgent”, and how many lines changed. People sort by those when they have nothing better. Docket knows how long each one takes and who’s waiting.

Still open

  1. Your fixes or their reviews? A conflict on yours sits below a review someone is blocked on, since your agent can often start on yours. Flipping it gets yours merged sooner.
  2. How much time by default. 30 minutes, then whatever you picked last.
  3. Notifications. Only a new row in Quick notifies you, so a ping always means a minute or two of work.
